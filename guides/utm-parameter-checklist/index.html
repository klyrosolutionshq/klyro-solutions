GUIDE / EMAIL CAMPAIGN QA

How to check UTM parameters before sending an email campaign.

A practical pre-send checklist for spotting inconsistent campaign tags, duplicate parameters and accidental staging destinations in campaign exports.

Educational checklist · Review actual campaign requirements with your team
01 / THE BASICS

What are UTM parameters?

UTM parameters are labels appended to a destination URL so reporting tools can attribute visits to marketing sources and campaigns. The common fields are utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign. Some teams additionally use utm_content and utm_term.

For example, a fictional newsletter link might look like this:

https://example.com/offer?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=autumn_launch

Even if the destination path is correct, inconsistent parameter values can make campaign analysis harder. Your organization should choose an agreed naming convention before launch.

1. Verify the required tags

Check every campaign link against your agreed requirements. A newsletter may require source, medium and campaign, while account or utility links may be intentionally exempt.

  • Is the expected utm_source value present?
  • Is utm_medium consistent, such as email rather than mixed alternatives?
  • Does utm_campaign use the exact agreed campaign identifier?

2. Check naming consistency

Review spelling, separators, spaces and letter case. For example, Autumn_Launch and autumn_launch may be reported as different values by some analytics tools. Use the naming rules approved for your own system.

3. Look for duplicate parameters

A URL that repeats the same key can be ambiguous, especially when values disagree. Example:

https://example.com/offer?utm_source=email&utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email

Prefer one intended value per key unless the target system documents a reason otherwise.

4. Check destinations for placeholders and staging domains

Review URLs containing terms like staging, preview or obvious placeholder hostnames. A static pattern check is a warning to investigate, not proof that a link is broken.

5. Validate the live behavior separately

Open important links in an appropriate testing environment and independently verify redirect behavior, destination content, analytics collection and email rendering. A static UTM checker does not prove these activities work.

6. Keep a review record

Document which links were checked, which exceptions were intentional, what you fixed and who approved the campaign. Exported findings help preserve a review trail.

Copyable pre-send checklist

  • Confirm campaign naming policy and exempt links.
  • Scan every campaign file for expected UTMs.
  • Review duplicate and inconsistent tracking values.
  • Investigate likely staging and placeholder URLs.
  • Test critical destinations and redirects separately.
  • Verify analytics collection in your intended system.
  • Export and sign off findings before the campaign is sent.
